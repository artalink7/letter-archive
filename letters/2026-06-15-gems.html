🎬 Weekly Movie

For this week's movie pick, I have chosen Kill Bill: The Whole Bloody Affair directed by Quentin Tarantino.

Release poster.

...Yes! The volume 1 / 2 bullshit has officially ended 🙂

Just recently, Quentin Tarantino gained the rights to combine Kill Bill: Volume 1 and Kill Bill Volume: 2 into a single movie, as he originally planned.

During the production of the movie, the infamous film producer Harvey Weinstein fought against Tarantino for splitting the long cut into two parts (the Volumes 1 and 2).

Producers and ditribution executives have always hated long movies: the risk that people might be bored and consequently avoid the movie theater is too high.

Weinstein eventually convinced Tarantino about dividing his 4 hours and 30 minutes movie into two different parts. Yes, it was a real crime. Not Weinstein's worst crime though... 💀 here the worst

However, we can finally enjoy the four and a half hours movie! And you might be surprised to know that this "new" cut is not just the union of the two volumes:

1) The animated part regarding the origin of O-Ren Ishii (code name Cottonmouth) presents a new sequence, making the whole animation part considerably longer.

A young Cottonmouth

2) The "plot twist" at the end of Volume 1 (I cannot spoil) is cut, and we come to know about it only at the end of the movie.

3) There's a post-credit animated scene that was made in collaboration with the video game Fortnite. You can also find this new "chapter" on Fortnite youtube channel 👇🏻

Here some of the reasons why I love Kill Bill :

- Fights. All the combat scenes are absolutely amazing. The best one is probably the Bride vs the Crazy 88...

The Bride extracts an eye from one of the Crazy 88

... which culminates in the fight between the Bride and O-Ren Ishii

Black mamba (Uma Thurman, aka the Bride) vs Cottonmouth (Lucy Liu, aka O-Ren Ishii)

- References. There are lots of gems you can find, as you dig deeper and deeper... 💎
The most evident reference is the iconic yellow suit of the bride. The whole movie is basically a citation of old Asian combat movies. The yellow suite comes from the Hong Kong movie "The Game of Death", starring Bruce Lee.

Bruce Lee (left) and Uma Thurman (right).

Another incredible reference is in the soundtrack. As in most of its movies, Tarantino does not use original music. In this case, one of the themes comes from an old italian movie called "Sette note in nero" (Seven Dark Notes), directed by Lucio Fulci.
Fulci is often credited by Tarantino as one of its biggest inspiration. Also, the theme song is actually made of seven notes...

Theme song from "Sette Note in Nero", by Lucio Fulci. The same song is mixed into the soundtrack of Kill Bill

- Soundtrack. All the tracks in the movie are mixed by RZA, the leader of the hip hop crew Wu-Tang Clan. The soundtracks mixes Ennio Morricone' scores, as well as Quincy Jones and Nancy Sinatra.

🎵 Weekly song

Liquid Swords by GZA

🎧 Liquid Swords on Spotify  •  ▶️ YouTube

When the bride finally confronts Bill, at the end of the movie, there is a moment when she is laid on a bed and the TV is on. She starts to watch a movie called "Shogun Assassination". We hear a monologue of a kid from that movie, whose background story is similar to the story of the bride in Kill Bill.

This same monologue was already sampled and used in the song Liquid Swords by GZA, from 1995.

GZA is a member of the Wu-Tang clan, whose leader is the already mentioned RZA. I guess they didn't really make an effort in choosing the names...

Anyway, the song is a real hip-hop classic. The beat is simple and effective. Naturally the lyrics are about violence and drugs:
